# Lactation Consultant: In-Person vs Remote Appointments — Questions to Ask

Choosing between an in-person and a remote lactation consultant appointment is not about which format is universally better. It is about which format fits your situation, your questions, and what you and your baby need support with right now. This guide offers a practical way to think it through, a list of questions to ask, and a simple checklist you can use before you book.

This article is general information for people researching lactation consultant services. It is not medical, legal, or individualized treatment advice. For concerns about you or your baby's health, feeding, weight, or development, consult qualified professionals who can assess your specific situation.

## Why the Format Matters

The format of an appointment shapes what a lactation consultant can observe, what you can practice together, and how follow-up is planned. Both in-person and remote appointments can be useful. They simply offer different kinds of information and hands-on opportunity.

In general terms:

- **In-person appointments** allow for direct observation and hands-on guidance in the same room.
- **Remote appointments** allow for conversation, observation over video, and guidance you can try at home, often with less travel and scheduling friction.

Neither format is automatically more effective for every family. The better choice depends on the questions you want answered and the kind of support you are looking for.

## Questions to Ask Yourself Before You Choose

Start with your own situation. These questions help you clarify what you need from the appointment.

1. **What is the main question I want help with?** For example, is it about positioning, routine, comfort, or something else?
2. **Would it help to have someone watch a feeding in person, or would a video call work for my question?**
3. **How much travel time and disruption can I realistically manage right now?**
4. **Do I have a private, quiet space for a video appointment?**
5. **Do I have reliable internet and a device with a camera?**
6. **Is there anyone at home who can help during the appointment?**
7. **What follow-up would I want, and how would that work in each format?**
8. **Are there any practical or safety considerations that make one format easier for me?**

There are no right or wrong answers here. The goal is to match the format to your circumstances.

## Questions to Ask a Prospective Lactation Consultant

When you contact a provider, whether by phone, email, or a directory listing, you can ask questions that help you compare formats. The exact answers will vary by provider and location, so treat these as prompts for a conversation rather than a script with fixed answers.

**About the appointment itself**

- What does a typical in-person appointment involve?
- What does a typical remote appointment involve?
- How long are appointments usually, and is there time for questions?
- What technology or platform do you use for remote visits, and what do I need to have ready?
- Are there any parts of an appointment that work better in person for the kind of question I have?

**About observation and hands-on guidance**

- In a remote appointment, how do you observe a feeding or a practice session?
- In an in-person appointment, what kinds of things can we try together?
- If something is hard to assess remotely, what would you suggest as a next step?

**About follow-up and communication**

- How is follow-up handled after an in-person appointment?
- How is follow-up handled after a remote appointment?
- What is the best way to reach you with a quick question afterward?
- If we need to switch formats, how does that work?

**About fit and logistics**

- What should I have ready before the appointment?
- Is there anything I should note down beforehand?
- What is your approach to working with other professionals involved in my care?

If a provider cannot answer a question, that is useful information too. A clear "I'm not sure, let me find out" can be more helpful than an overly confident answer.

## A Practical Checklist

Use this checklist to organize your decision. You can copy it into a note and fill it in as you gather information.

**Before booking**

- [ ] I have written down my main question or concern.
- [ ] I have noted any practical constraints (travel, time, privacy, internet).
- [ ] I have decided whether in-person, remote, or either format could work.
- [ ] I have a short list of providers to contact.
- [ ] I have prepared my questions.

**When contacting a provider**

- [ ] I asked what each format involves.
- [ ] I asked how observation and hands-on guidance work in each format.
- [ ] I asked how follow-up is handled.
- [ ] I asked what I need to prepare.
- [ ] I noted any answers that were unclear or that I want to follow up on.

**Before the appointment**

- [ ] I know the time, format, and how to join or where to go.
- [ ] I have a quiet, private space if the appointment is remote.
- [ ] I have my device, camera, and internet ready if the appointment is remote.
- [ ] I have my notes and questions handy.
- [ ] I know how to reach the provider if something changes.

**After the appointment**

- [ ] I noted what we discussed and any suggested next steps.
- [ ] I know how and when to follow up.
- [ ] I know who to contact with new or urgent concerns.

This checklist is a planning tool, not a substitute for professional advice.

## Clearly Labeled Examples

The following are hypothetical examples, clearly labeled as examples. They are not real cases, not research findings, and not success stories. They are included only to illustrate how someone might think through the decision.

**Example 1: A parent who wants to practice a new position**

A parent is curious about trying a different feeding position and wants someone to watch and offer suggestions in the moment. They might lean toward an in-person appointment because hands-on guidance and direct observation could be useful. They would still ask the provider how they handle questions that come up later.

**Example 2: A parent with limited travel time**

A parent has a busy schedule and limited travel options. They might consider a remote appointment first, because it could reduce travel and make it easier to fit into the day. They would ask how the provider observes a feeding over video and what happens if something is hard to see.

**Example 3: A parent who is unsure which format fits**

A parent is not sure whether their question needs in-person observation. They might contact a provider and describe their situation in general terms, then ask which format the provider would suggest and why. The provider's reasoning can help the parent decide.

**Example 4: A parent planning follow-up**

A parent expects they may want follow-up support. They might ask how follow-up works in each format before booking, so they can choose a format that fits their longer-term plan.

In each example, the decision comes from the parent's own circumstances and questions, not from a claim that one format is always better.

## How to Compare Without Overlooking Follow-Up

It is easy to focus on the first appointment and forget what comes after. When you compare options, ask about follow-up in the same conversation. Follow-up might look different in each format, and knowing that in advance can help you plan.

You may also want to think about how you would handle a change in circumstances. If a remote appointment turns out not to fit your needs, what would the next step be? If an in-person appointment raises a new question, how would you get support? These are reasonable questions to ask any provider.

## Keeping the Decision in Perspective

Choosing between in-person and remote appointments is a practical decision, not a test. You can change your mind, try a different format later, or combine formats over time. The important thing is that you feel informed about what each option involves and that you can ask questions without pressure.

General information can help you plan, but it cannot assess your specific situation. If you have concerns about feeding, comfort, weight, or your baby's health, consult qualified professionals. They can look at your circumstances directly and help you decide on next steps.
