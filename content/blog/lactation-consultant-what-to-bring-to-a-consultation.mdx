# Lactation Consultant: What to Bring to a Consultation

Preparing for a lactation consultant consultation can feel like packing for a trip you have never taken before. You may not know what matters, what is optional, and what you will wish you had remembered once you are sitting in the room. This guide offers a practical checklist and some clearly labeled hypothetical examples so you can walk in feeling organized rather than overwhelmed.

This article is general information, not medical, legal, or individualized treatment advice. It does not diagnose any condition or promise any outcome. For questions about your own health or your baby's health, consult qualified professionals.

## Why Preparation Makes a Difference

A lactation consultant consultation is usually a focused conversation combined with observation and hands-on guidance. The more context you can share, the more efficiently the consultant can understand your situation and discuss options with you. Preparation is not about having the right answers. It is about bringing the right information so you do not have to rely on memory alone.

Think of it as gathering ingredients before cooking. You can always improvise, but having things within reach tends to make the process smoother.

## Core Checklist: Items and Information to Bring

Use this checklist as a starting point. Not every item will apply to every family, and that is okay. Check off what is relevant and leave the rest.

### 1. Feeding Log or Simple Notes

If you have been tracking feedings in any form, bring that record. It does not need to be perfect or digital. A few notes on your phone or a sheet of paper with times and general observations can be helpful. If you have not been tracking, that is fine too. You can bring a short written summary instead.

Consider noting:

- Roughly how often feeding sessions have been happening
- How long they tend to last
- Anything you have noticed about comfort, positioning, or your baby's behavior

### 2. Questions You Want to Ask

Write down your top three to five questions before the appointment. This is one of the most useful things you can bring. When you are in the moment, it is easy to forget what you meant to ask.

### 3. Relevant Products You Are Already Using

If you are using any pumps, shields, bottles, or other feeding-related products, consider bringing them or photos of them. The consultant may be able to discuss how you are using them and whether adjustments might be worth exploring.

### 4. A Support Person, If You Would Like One

You are welcome to bring a partner, family member, or friend if that would help you feel supported. A second set of ears can be useful for remembering details afterward. If you prefer to attend alone, that is equally valid.

### 5. Comfort Items for You and Your Baby

Consultations can take time. Bringing water, a snack, a burp cloth, a spare diaper, and anything that helps you and your baby feel comfortable is practical and worthwhile.

### 6. Any Written Information You Already Have

If you have received written plans, discharge summaries, or notes from other providers, bring copies if you have them. This can help the consultant understand what has already been discussed. You are always in control of what you choose to share.

### 7. A Way to Take Notes

Your phone, a notebook, or a tablet can help you capture what you discuss. Some consultants also provide written summaries. If you would like one, it is reasonable to ask.

## Hypothetical Examples: What Preparation Can Look Like

The following scenarios are hypothetical and clearly labeled as examples. They are not real client stories, success stories, or evidence of any particular result. They are simply illustrations of how preparation might look in different situations.

### Example A: The First-Time Parent

Imagine a parent who has never worked with a lactation consultant before. They are not sure what to expect. Before the appointment, they write down three questions: what to expect during the visit, how to know if feeding is going well, and what follow-up might look like. They bring a notebook, a water bottle, and a photo of the pump they are using at home.

This is a simple, low-pressure way to prepare. The goal is not to have everything figured out. The goal is to have a starting point for the conversation.

### Example B: The Parent With a Busy Schedule

Imagine a parent who is balancing work and caregiving. They have limited time and want to make the most of the appointment. Before the visit, they type a short list of concerns on their phone and note the times of day when feeding tends to feel most challenging. They bring their phone, a charger, and a support person who can help remember details.

This approach focuses on efficiency without adding stress. The notes do not need to be elaborate.

### Example C: The Parent Who Feels Overwhelmed

Imagine a parent who feels unsure where to begin. They decide to bring only two things: a list of their top questions and a willingness to ask for clarification when needed. They tell the consultant at the start that they are feeling overwhelmed and would appreciate a simple explanation.

This is a valid and thoughtful way to prepare. You are allowed to set the pace and ask for information to be repeated or explained in plain language.

## What You Do Not Need to Bring

It can be just as helpful to know what is not required. You do not need to bring a perfect feeding log, a complete medical history, or a set of polished questions. You do not need to have tried every product on the market. You do not need to apologize for where you are starting.

A consultation is a conversation, not an exam. The consultant's role is to listen, observe, and discuss options with you. Your role is to share what you are comfortable sharing.

## Questions to Ask Before the Consultation

When you are selecting a lactation consultant, you may want to ask a few logistical questions in advance. These are general examples, not requirements:

- What should I bring to the appointment?
- How long does a typical consultation last?
- Is there anything I should do to prepare?
- What does follow-up usually look like?
- How do you prefer to communicate between visits, if needed?

Once the appointment is over, you may want to review any notes you took and any written information you received. If something is unclear, it is reasonable to ask a follow-up question. ## Final Thoughts

Bringing the right items and information to a lactation consultant consultation is less about perfection and more about preparation. A short list of questions, a few notes, and a willingness to ask for what you need can go a long way. Use the checklist above as a flexible guide, and adapt it to your own situation.
